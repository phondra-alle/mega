Two mechanisms described by the export

Shared parser

The supplied logic says the shared parser reads numeric dates month-first while the tenant uses day-first dates. R-41200 describes 7 April becoming 4 July; R-41203 describes 2 December becoming 12 February.

The required outcome is agreement between the intended date and the application’s interpretation. This export does not include the production parser.

booking-v3 form hint

The supplied form hint says MM/DD/YYYY, but the form reads input in the tenant’s day-first order. A person following that instruction can obtain the wrong date. Examples include R-41230, R-41241 and R-41251.

This mechanism applies to form submissions for the relevant booking-v3 tenants. It is not established merely by a matching subject, browser locale, or intake tag. The displayed instruction and the input interpretation must agree.

A safer triage proposal

The accepted subject-only decision and triage instructions are historical context, not proof that these reports share a cause. The decision remains unchanged.

Before merging, compare the tenant configuration, input channel, date-field instruction, and reported symptom. Different subjects may describe the same mechanism; the same subject may describe different mechanisms. Unknown tenant context needs investigation rather than automatic merging.

For the exceptional reports, obtain the intended date/time, original calendar payload, displayed result, timezone settings, and delivery timestamps. Timezone conversion is a hypothesis for some symptoms, not a confirmed common cause. Split the investigation when evidence identifies separate defects.
